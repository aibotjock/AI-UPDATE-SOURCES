# Source quality and evolving technology coverage

Every episode must use the repository's technology-source registry and relevant media/evidence library. Only reputable, reliable sources that are updated regularly qualify for active episode use. Use the registry as a discovery and continuity index; verify original claim content and source maintenance on the episode's research day.

## Regular updates are required

Confirm an observable update pattern using dated releases, revisions, feeds, version history, changelogs or maintained leaderboard updates. Record the observed cadence, a maintenance-history URL or retained evidence, the latest update date when available and the maintenance-review timestamp. Do not invent a cadence, treat today's retrieval as an update, or infer regular maintenance merely from a familiar publisher or live URL.

Use regularly maintained official release/documentation collections, transparent ongoing benchmark publications, actively revised technical references and reputable technology outlets with recurring updates. A cited result can retain its older measurement/publication date when the maintained source still presents it as applicable; source maintenance does not make an old result a new measurement.

Static, abandoned, irregularly maintained or maintenance-unverified sources are ineligible for episode facts until verified or replaced with a qualifying source. A standalone older paper or saved snapshot does not itself prove regular source updates. Preserve it as historical evidence where permitted, but obtain current support from a qualifying maintained source before using it in new episode reporting.

The starter registry records remain candidates: regular_updates_verified is false and cadence/history fields are empty until an actual review. Do not silently promote them. An episode source review records both source-maintenance eligibility and the specific content claim it supports.

## Select sources appropriate to the claim

Prefer official releases, availability documentation, pricing, model cards, technical reports, benchmark maintainers, original research and transparent independent evaluations. Reputable reporting can support context when traced to its original evidence.

Evaluate identifiable authors/publishers, first-hand access to the subject, methodology and disclosed conditions, publication/measurement dates, reproducibility, correction practices, incentives and exact claim support. Reliability is claim-specific and can change. A provider can authoritatively describe its own API price while its performance claims remain company-reported evidence. A popular or official page is not automatically independent validation.

Search snippets, summaries, promotional posts and unsourced speculation are discovery aids. Open the relevant original page or supplied authorized record. Verify exact model/version, tools/effort, benchmark version, date, units, access, cost conditions and limits. Mark unknown fields rather than guessing.

Use independent evidence for consequential or disputed conclusions when available. If only vendor evidence is available, attribute it and limit the conclusion. Investigate conflicts; do not average incompatible scores or invent agreement. Prefer fewer directly relevant strong sources over redundant weak coverage; no source-count quota.

## Registry lifecycle

Keep each source's stable ID, canonical URL, publisher, claim role, reliability rationale, access status and review dates. A candidate is available for investigation, not certified trustworthy for every statement. Distinguish candidate, active, disputed, inaccessible and retired statuses as appropriate, with reasons and last review scope.

Before using a source, review its actual content for the claim. An HTTP success or accessible URL does not set content_claims_reviewed to true. Record completed claim verification in the episode ledger with the source URL, evidence passage/data location, reviewer, publication/measurement date, retrieval timestamp and limitations. Registry-level review scope may remain narrow even after individual claims are checked.

Add or replace a source when it improves verifiability, coverage, timeliness or methodological quality. Record the old and new IDs/URLs, reason, date and affected claims. Mark retired records rather than deleting history. Preserve the old evidence used in prior episodes and flag dependent recommendations when a source changes or a correction is needed.

Do not freeze model lists, benchmark leaders or publisher reputations. Reassess the relevant roster each production day. Historical charts retain their original dates; verify that reused benchmarks remain the latest relevant compatible results. A newly retrieved old source does not become a new measurement.

## Media and exact graphics

Use library/asset-index.json and library/INDEX.md for selection, then check relevant provenance and reuse restrictions. Save unchanged captures where permitted and separate annotations. Draw exact charts from retained verified numerical records. Generated chip artwork, templates and decorative imagery are not benchmark, interface or test evidence.

Keep access limitations visible. Do not purchase access, bypass restrictions or invent source checks. Select a suitable authorized alternative or report a specific evidence gap.
