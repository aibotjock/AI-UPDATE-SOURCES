# Source quality and evolving technology coverage

Every episode must use the repository's technology-source registry and relevant media/evidence library. Only reputable, reliable sources that are updated regularly qualify for active episode use. Record the observed cadence, a maintenance-history URL or retained evidence, the latest update date when available and the maintenance-review timestamp. Do not invent a cadence, treat today's retrieval as an update, or infer regular maintenance merely from a familiar publisher or live URL.

Use regularly maintained official release/documentation collections, transparent ongoing benchmark publications, actively revised technical references and reputable technology outlets with recurring updates. A cited result can retain its older measurement/publication date when the maintained source still presents it as applicable; source maintenance does not make an old result a new measurement.

Static, abandoned, irregularly maintained or maintenance-unverified sources are ineligible for episode facts until verified or replaced with a qualifying source. A standalone older paper or saved snapshot does not itself prove regular source updates. Preserve it as historical evidence where permitted, but obtain current support from a qualifying maintained source before using it in new episode reporting.

The starter registry records remain candidates: regular_updates_verified is false and cadence/history fields are empty until an actual review. Do not silently promote them. An episode source review records both source-maintenance eligibility and the specific content claim it supports.

## Select sources appropriate to the claim

Prefer official releases, availability documentation, pricing, model cards, technical reports, benchmark maintainers, original research and transparent independent evaluations. Reputable reporting can support context when traced to its original evidence.

Evaluate identifiable authors/publishers, first-hand access to the subject, methodology and disclosed conditions, publication/measurement dates, reproducibility, correction practices, incentives and exact claim support. Reliability is claim-specific and can change. A provider can authoritatively describe its own API price while its performance claims remain company-reported evidence. A popular or official page is not automatically independent validation.

Search snippets, summaries, promotional posts and unsourced speculation are discovery aids. Open the relevant original page or supplied authorized record. Verify exact model/version, tools/effort, benchmark version, date, units, access, cost conditions and limits. Mark unknown fields rather than guessing.

Use independent evidence for consequential or disputed conclusions when available. If only vendor evidence is available, attribute it and limit the conclusion. Keep each source's stable ID, canonical URL, publisher, claim role, reliability rationale, access status and review dates.
