AI UPDATE EPISODE FORMULA
Prepared October 12, 2026
Scope: this project and this thread only

PURPOSE

Use a fixed five-part sequence with a 12-minute default. Keep the audience questions and chapter names consistent while changing the stories, models, comparison tasks and time allocation to match current verified evidence. Help viewers choose the best supported cloud LLM or platform for their task and budget. Cover relevant major-player and emerging technologies when they affect those choices.

Every full episode runs 10–15 minutes, never under ten. Exceed fifteen only rarely, with a documented editorial reason. The intended release cadence is every other day.

RECOGNIZABLE EPISODE STRUCTURE

1. THE MAIN TAKEAWAY — 00:00–00:30
Audience question: What will I learn today?
Lead with a useful verified finding or a concrete viewer question. Identify AI UPDATE, the episode date and its research cutoff. Episode 01 uses a 35–45-second channel introduction; later episodes introduce the day's finding briefly. Move time between segments to accommodate that opening.

2. WHAT CHANGED — 00:30–03:15
Audience question: What is new, and why does it matter?
Explain consequential releases, platform changes, price/access changes and relevant emerging technology. Separate available releases from announced plans and attribution from verification. Check all core companies but choose screen time by significance; retain comprehensive coverage in the dated index. A company without a verified update does not need an artificial story.

3. THE BENCHMARK SCOREBOARD — 03:15–05:45
Audience question: Which models lead, and what do the scores actually mean?
Present the latest relevant comparable benchmark evidence. Identify overall point leaders within named evaluations, relevant category leaders and within-platform alternatives, with dates, configurations and limitations. Choose readable examples for screen time and retain broader comparisons in the index. The first episode teaches benchmark purposes and interpretation more deeply. Do not invent a universal winner.

4. THE SAME-PROMPT COMPARISON — 05:45–09:45
Audience question: What do these models actually produce for the money?
Show genuine outputs from the same task, predefined success criteria, exact model/provider/configuration and total task cost. Explain the prompt, results and cost in readable scenes, then hold a combined panel containing prompt, actual outputs, model/provider, cost and compatible cited benchmark evidence. Distinguish this practical test from published benchmark results. Preserve full inputs/outputs, failures/retries and cost records; never display an answer key as actual model output. Actual comparative results remain required in every episode; they serve model selection and do not require a platform how-to lesson.

5. YOUR CHOICE AND WHAT IS NEXT — 09:45–12:00
Audience question: Which option fits my task and budget?
Give narrow evidence-backed recommendations, explain quality/cost tradeoffs and important uncertainty, and summarize confirmed developments to watch. End with a clear practical takeaway. Identify an overall leader only where comparable evidence supports it; explain unresolved differences or missing evidence honestly.

OPTIONAL PRACTICAL LESSON — SPECIAL OCCASIONS ONLY

Reserve a practical cloud-platform lesson for exceptional items: a verified best-in-class feature or a verified new feature with substantial viewer value. It has no routine slot in standard episodes. Include one only when the item merits special coverage, and redistribute time from the five standard sections while retaining their roles and the 10–15-minute format.

When included, demonstrate genuine input and result and show the steps, required access/plan/region, limits and common mistakes. Distinguish new features from established ones selected for exceptional value. A claimed demonstration must have actual evidence, not a fabricated interface or unperformed workflow. Standard episode completion does not depend on a practical lesson or its feature-demonstration evidence.

FLEXIBILITY RULES

Keep the five chapter roles recognizable. Shift minutes toward the most consequential development or best comparison example; the times above are a baseline, not quotas. Preserve benchmark evidence and a genuine combined comparison in every full episode. Add a practical lesson only under the special-occasion rule above.

On quiet news days, deepen supported benchmark interpretation, practical comparisons and task/value recommendations. Report the absence of consequential verified change instead of inventing news or padding runtime. A quiet news day does not by itself justify adding a practical lesson.

Necessary recurring items may repeat across episodes, including the latest benchmarks until replaced, relevant existing comparisons and definitions. Keep original measurement/publication dates and label unchanged results accurately. Recheck whether reused evidence remains applicable and current. Any repeated practical lesson must still meet the special-occasion rule.

Reuse actual test evidence when still applicable. Rerun affected tests when model/version, configuration, tools, task inputs or evaluation criteria change enough to invalidate the comparison. If prices change, distinguish the original measured bill from an updated cost estimate; do not relabel an old test as a new run.

Monitor OpenAI, Anthropic/Claude, Google, Microsoft, Meta, NVIDIA's relevant cloud offerings, xAI/Grok, DeepSeek, Z.ai/GLM and Alibaba/Qwen, plus relevant influential offerings. Broad monitoring does not require equal screen time or a fixed count of stories. Keep editorial attention on cloud models/platforms and practical task value.

SOURCE ELIGIBILITY

Use the repository source registry and media library for every episode. Only reputable, reliable and regularly updated sources qualify; verify and record their observable maintenance history/cadence. Do not assume listed sources have passed that review. Retire or replace sources when maintenance, relevance or reliability deteriorates, preserving historical evidence and reasons. Follow channel/source-review-policy.md.

FRESHNESS AND ACCURACY

Generate each episode prompt for the actual local production day in America/New_York. Record episode ID/title, prompt date, actual script creation/finalization date, same-day research/verification date and exact cutoff timestamp. Source publication and benchmark measurement dates are separate fields.

Complete same-day research and claim verification before writing narration. Inspect original sources and retain a claim-level evidence ledger. Cite applicable claims in visuals, script/notes and the prepared video description. Distinguish vendor claims, independent evaluations, channel tests, estimates, analysis and uncertainty.

Recheck material changeable claims at finalization. If finalization moves to a new calendar day, refresh those claims for the new date. If publication is delayed, refresh time-sensitive coverage before release and update affected narration/visuals and the date block as needed. State currentness as of the disclosed cutoff.

Never invent missing updates, scores, prices, outputs, features, source checks or playback results. Resolve genuine gaps with authorized access or report the specific blocker.

DAILY EPISODE PROMPT FIELDS

Each new prompt contains:
- The date block, episode number/title and main viewer question.
- What changed, what remains current and what has been superseded.
- Selected compatible benchmark evidence and source/claim IDs.
- The practical comparison, success criteria and actual evidence requirements.
- An optional practical lesson and its evidence/access requirements only when a verified best or new item merits special coverage.
- Adapted five-part timing and scene plan, including any recurring segments and time redistributed for an optional lesson.
- Selected assets from AI-UPDATE-SOURCES and its media-library collection.
- Required deliverables, verification checks and known blockers.

Do not hard-code today's model leaders, prices or headlines into the reusable master. Each daily prompt derives them from that day's checked evidence. Distinguish generating the episode prompt from executing its production; generating this formula does not publish content or authorize paid commitments.

EFFICIENT PRODUCTION AND FINISHED EPISODE

Use the existing source/asset indexes before loading relevant resources. Research and testing lead to a checked evidence packet, then the script and rough storyboard. Produce checked narration in reusable scene segments and measure its actual duration before final motion/caption timing.

Use consistent chapter labels, voice and visual identity. Plan animated charts/diagrams/timelines, pans/zooms/highlights, authentic logo reveals, demonstrations/recordings, useful B-roll and restrained sound cues. Select purposeful effects rather than forcing every option into every scene. Generate exact factual graphics from verified data, not generative imagery.

Keep one scene manifest, one claim ledger and one numeric dataset. Reuse still-valid assets/audio and rebuild affected scenes after updates, then inspect the assembled episode.

Deliver an actual narrated 1080p MP4, editable deck, timestamped Word/plain-text script, synchronized captions, audio stems/mix, sources/test records/cost calculations, comparison index and prepared description/chapters. Verify actual runtime, intelligible narration, synchronized motion/audio/captions, readable citations and complete playback.

The existing two-minute introduction preview is separate. A later 120-second demonstration excerpt comes from the settled full episode; neither replaces the ten-minute minimum.
