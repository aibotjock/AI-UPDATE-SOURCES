Important websites, articles, papers, release notes and benchmark pages must be shown on screen at the relevant narration cue. A final reference list or hidden Sources button alone is insufficient.
For each featured source:
- Show an authentic page/capture with readable publisher, title and publication date.
- Frame the relevant passage, then zoom and highlight exactly what the narration discusses.
- Preserve necessary qualifiers and context; never change source wording to strengthen the story.
- Keep source text separate from editorial labels, annotations and illustrative graphics.
- Retain full original URL and publication/capture dates in the evidence records and prepared video description.
- Link each capture, highlight and narration passage through source and claim IDs.
Prefer prepared dated captures for dependable recording; use live browsing selectively when it adds demonstrated value. Keep the original capture unchanged and save overlays/annotated derivatives separately. Record capture viewport/crop, locator or region, hash and permissions. Verify fonts, readable text, correct pages and absence of access-error banners.
If capture is unavailable, use an accurately attributed short excerpt or a factual chart derived from verified data and disclose the presentation form. Do not fabricate a website screenshot or imply a recreated excerpt is a capture. Respect access and reuse restrictions; do not bypass paywalls. Quote only necessary short passages.
The source panel should support the explanation rather than dominate every moment. Allow enough reading time and prepare a source-failure fallback for live demonstrations.
